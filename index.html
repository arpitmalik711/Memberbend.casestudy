01 Project Overview

Structural Remediation of BAJA ATV Chassis Member

During high-G suspension testing, a primary chassis member experienced plastic deformation and weld-zone cracking at the rear damper mounting point under a 4g load case — caused by a fundamental load-path geometry error.

I performed a correlation study between 1D analytical beam equations and 3D FEA in ANSYS, then implemented a triangulated load path redirection — converting bending into axial tension/compression, reducing peak stress by 0%.

03 Assumptions vs Reality

What Was Designed vs What Happened

The damper mount was placed mid-span on a primary chassis tube — geometrically correct for suspension kinematics, but analytically flawed in structural load path.

☑ Assumed: Load Path
Mount placed 9.5 in away from Node 1 for correct damper angle and stroke geometry.
Assumed: Member carries load axially — efficient use of tubular section.
✕ Reality: Load Path
Mid-span transverse load creates bending moment M = F·a(L−a)/L. Axial strength of tube is irrelevant — bending dominates entirely.
☑ Assumed: Stress Level
Initial design check estimated stresses within safe envelope.
Assumed FOS ≥ 2.0 per Baja SAE structural guidelines.
✕ Reality: Stress Level
Bending + weld-toe Kt drives σVM to 1031 MPa.
Actual FOS = 0.74 — structural failure was mathematically certain.
☑ Assumed: Material Margin
Sult = 855 MPa assumed sufficient for the design load envelope with standard safety factor applied.
✕ Reality: Material Margin
Calculated stress exceeds Sult by 31.4%. Material substitution alone cannot fix a load-path geometry error of this magnitude.
▶ Key Insight
This was not a material failure. It was a geometry of load path failure. The correct fix is therefore geometric: redirect loads through triangulation — not material upgrade.

05 Root Cause Analysis

Why the Failure Occurred

Force decomposition under the 4g load case precisely isolated the failure mechanism. The mid-span mount placement converted the chassis tube into a simply-supported beam under concentrated loading — worst possible geometry for a tube.

◈ Analytical Method — Euler–Bernoulli Beam Theory
(1) Decompose damper axis force into transverse & axial components at mount node under 4g.
(2) Free-body diagram of chassis member.
(3) Bending moment: M = F·a(L−a)/L (peak at mount, a = 9.5 in from Node 1).
(4) Bending stress: σb = M·c/I (extreme fibre); τ from shear; σaxial = F∥/Apipe.
(5) Von Mises: σVM = √(σ² + 3τ²) where σ = σaxial + σb → 1031 MPa.
(6) FOS = Syield/σVM = 763/1031 = 0.74 — failure certain.

09 Engineering Decision

Triangulated Load Path Redirection

The fix targets the root cause — not the symptom. Rather than upsizing the original member, I eliminated the bending moment by adding a triangulating member from the damper mount node to an adjacent frame node.

✕ Rejected: Upsize Original Member
Increasing OD or wall thickness reduces stress but does not eliminate bending. The load-path geometry remains flawed. Adds weight. Does not address root cause.
✓ Chosen: Triangulation
New member welded from mount node to adjacent chassis node. All members become two-force (axial only). Bending moment M → ≈ 0. Stress drops 0%. Minimal weight impact.
◈ Why Triangulation Eliminates Bending
In a triangulated truss node, every member intersects at a common point. A load at the node resolves into pure axial forces in all connected members — no moment arm, no bending. The eccentricity e (which drove M = F·e in the original mid-span mount) is reduced to zero by routing load through the joint directly.
